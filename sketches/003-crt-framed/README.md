## Variant: CRT, framed (appliance)

### Design stance
It is a machine you sit in front of, not a document you read. The screen has a bezel, the memory has a shape, and the chrome is allowed to have presence.

### Key choices
- Layout: a top bar (session, view bytes, node count, cache, jobs), a 30ch rail, the turn, and a footer of file paths. Boxed panels with 3px radii.
- Typography: 13px, 1.6 leading; the reasoning sits in its own bordered box rather than a gutter.
- Colour: the same cold blue, pushed a little further — a scanline overlay at 2% and an edge vignette, exit codes in boxed chips, amber for `work`.
- Katakana markers (`記憶`, `窓`) on two rail labels as texture. **This is the one deliberate risk in the set** — if it reads as costume rather than texture, it is two characters to delete.
- Interaction: the thought box folds, tool output unfolds, hover states on the palette row.

### Trade-offs
- Strong at: the "I am inside a wired thing" feeling; the tree reads as a shape, not a table; the frame gives the eye somewhere to rest.
- Weak at: the bezel and texture cost vertical room; scanlines are a love-it-or-hate-it; it is the least calm of the three over a long session.

### Best for
- The register you described: Lain's cold blue room. Choose this if you want the tool to *feel* like the thing, not just work like it.
